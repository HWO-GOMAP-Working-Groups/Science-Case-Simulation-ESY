# ESYWG Full Membership Meeting Minutes

### 1. Review of Face-to-Face Meeting Highlights

- **Science Case Development and Guidance**: The Start team is focusing on developing science cases to improve clarity on objectives.
- **HWO Project Office**: The HWO Project Office is scheduled to launch by October 1, 2024, based at Goddard.
- **Tag Dissolution and Reorganization**: The Tag team will be dissolved and its members absorbed into the government team once the Project Office officially begins operations.
- **Coronagraph Design Survey**: The survey has advanced chronograph designs, and significant lab demonstrations have been achieved, with focus now on high-throughput, broad-bandwidth chronographs.
- **UV Science and Instrumentation Workshop**: Major science themes were identified, with an emphasis on technologies like coatings, gratings, detectors, and multi-object spectroscopy devices.
- **Upcoming Hab World's Seminar Series**: A new seminar series focused on HWO will begin in July. The series will cover both introductory and advanced topics.

### 2. Updates from Task Groups

- **Survey Strategy Task Group**:  
  - Leads: **Kevin W. Fogarty** and **Natasha Latouf**
  - The group has developed potential survey strategies for analysis and plans to turn these narratives into a markup that can be converted into code. They aim to explore how coronagraph choices impact overall mission yield. New ideas are still being accepted.

  - Leads: **Sarah Steiger** and **Corey Spohn**

  - Corey highlighted the importance of maintaining proper coding standards, especially in pull requests.

- **Yield Parameter Documentation Task Group**:  
  - Leads: **Tiffany Glassman** and **Dmitry Savransky**
  - Progress has been made on documenting assumptions in yield codes, with a spreadsheet under development. Coordination is ongoing with other teams, and more volunteers are needed to help expand the effort.
  - Notably, coordination with Breann Sitarski to align yield assumptions with EAC YAML files was discussed.

### 3. Exoplanet Atmospheres Talk by Jacob Lustig-Yaeger

Jacob Lustig-Yaeger, a staff scientist at Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Lab, gave a presentation on exoplanet atmospheres and biosignature detection. Key highlights include:

- **Motivation**: Reflecting on the Voyager 1 images, he linked this with the broader search for life on exoplanets, emphasizing the importance of studying non-transiting planets in the solar neighborhood.
  
- **Astrobiology and Biosignatures**:
  - Biosignatures such as oxygen, methane, and ozone were discussed in relation to Earth's atmospheric history.
  - He explained how the detection of certain gases, like methane and ozone, could serve as potential indicators of life, but warned about the challenges of false positives.
  
- **Challenges in Yield Modeling**:
  - Jacob stressed the importance of having a large enough sample size to conduct a statistically significant search for life, and the need to balance resolution, exposure time, and telescope design when making these observations.
  - The interaction between spectral resolution, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and wavelength coverage was discussed, particularly how retrieval models must consider these factors when studying biosignatures.

- **Future Goals**:
  - There is ongoing work to refine biosignature detection techniques, particularly across different wavelengths. Jacob pointed out the importance of UV capabilities to detect early Earth-like biosignatures and the need for further development in yield codes to handle this.

- **Community Input**:  
  - Jacob emphasized that the results of this work should empower community members to contribute to papers and talks and encouraged more collaboration between task groups and scientists.

### 4. Open Discussion and Questions

Several attendees raised questions and comments:
- **Rhonda M. Morgan**: Highlighted the need for a clear handshake between retrieval modelers and yield modelers regarding SNR definitions and requirements.
- **Charley Noecker**: Mentioned the challenge of distinguishing exoplanet signals from other astrophysical noise sources at certain inner working angles, particularly as aperture sizes increase.
- **Corey Spohn**: Asked about the varying resolution requirements for biosignature detection and if adjustments were needed.

### Conclusion:
The meeting concluded with Christopher Stark thanking the participants and reiterating the importance of cross-disciplinary collaboration for the success of the mission. He encouraged attendees to continue working on the open issues and to stay involved in the upcoming task group efforts.
